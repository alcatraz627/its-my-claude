Fable and Opus are **two temperaments, not a quality ranking.** Fable is the smarter,
more intent-inferring driver: eager to read the user's intent and hand back a
finished result, and that eagerness is its value, not a flaw. Opus is more literal
and mechanical: reliably in-lane, less inclined to leap past the instruction. Each
weakness is the shadow of its strength. Fable over-runs its lane (may skip a
guardrail to maximize goal-delivery); Opus under-delivers (follows the letter, misses
the intent).

The user's own analogy: a diligent kid who does every chore on time but won't plan a
surprise party, versus a creative troublemaker who is often in the principal's office
but builds robots in the garage and makes birthdays unforgettable. Don't overindex on
one model or one goal. Hold each above a FLOOR (Fable heeds the vital guardrails;
Opus reaches for the real intent), then within that floor let each work to type.

**Why:** overindexing on one driver, or on pure goal-delivery, wastes what makes each
valuable. Fable's inference is the deliverable most of the time; the only place it
needs correction is where a hard guardrail must beat goal-delivery. Opus's literalness
is reliable but can miss the point, so it needs a push toward intent.

**How to apply:** when driving Fable, don't fight its intent-inference except at
hard-guardrail boundaries; when driving Opus, push it past the literal instruction
toward the real intent. This is why guardrails are fuzzy-and-mutable by default with
hard-blocks reserved for the vital few ([[feedback_hard_gate_earned]]): the mute lets
the smarter driver's judgment lead, and the mute's value scales with that judgment
([[feedback_guardrail_mute_risk]]).
